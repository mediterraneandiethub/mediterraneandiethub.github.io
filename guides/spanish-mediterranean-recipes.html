Spanish Mediterranean Recipes

Spanish cooking is vibrant, social, and deeply Mediterranean. It is built from olive oil, garlic, tomato, peppers, and saffron, and its most famous dishes — paella, gazpacho, tortilla, and tapas — are known around the world. This guide shares the classic Spanish recipes and how to cook them at home.

Paella

Paella is Spain's most famous dish, and it comes from Valencia, where it was originally made with rice, vegetables, and rabbit or chicken. The seafood version is the best known outside Spain. The key is the rice: a short-grain rice that absorbs stock without going mushy. Cook it in a wide, shallow pan, do not stir it once the stock is added, and let a crisp layer form on the bottom — the socarrat — which is the prized part. Use saffron for colour and flavour, and add seafood, vegetables, and beans. Paella is a dish for sharing, cooked in the pan and eaten from it.

Gazpacho and cold soups

Gazpacho, the cold tomato soup from Andalusia, is one of the healthiest dishes in the Mediterranean. Blend ripe tomatoes, cucumber, pepper, onion, garlic, olive oil, and vinegar until smooth, and chill it well. Serve with chopped vegetables on top. Salmorejo, a thicker version from Córdoba, is made with bread and topped with egg and ham. Ajo blanco, a cold almond and garlic soup, is another Andalusian classic. These soups are perfect for hot weather, and they are a good way to use ripe tomatoes.

Tortilla and patatas bravas

The Spanish tortilla — a thick omelette of potato, egg, and onion — is eaten at any time of day, as a meal, a snack, or a tapa. Cook the potatoes slowly in olive oil until tender, mix with beaten eggs, and cook in a pan until set, turning once. Patatas bravas are fried potato cubes served with a spicy tomato sauce and aioli, and they are a staple of tapas bars. Both dishes are simple, cheap, and satisfying, and they show how Spanish cooking turns humble ingredients into something special.

Tapas and eating Spanish style

Tapas are small dishes served with drinks, and they are the heart of Spanish social eating. Classic tapas include olives, cheese, jamón, patatas bravas, garlic prawns, and tortilla. A tapas meal is a shared, leisurely affair, with several small dishes on the table and everyone helping themselves. To eat Spanish style at home, make three or four tapas dishes, serve them with bread and a salad, and take your time. The point is the company as much as the food.

Spanish food beyond paella

Spanish cooking goes far beyond paella. A fabada, a bean stew from Asturias, is a hearty winter dish. A salmorejo, a thick tomato soup from Córdoba, is a summer favourite. Pisto, a ratatouille-like dish of peppers, courgette, and tomato, is a versatile side or main. Romesco sauce, made from almonds, peppers, and garlic, is a Catalan classic that goes with fish, vegetables, and bread. These dishes are regional, seasonal, and deeply satisfying, and they show that Spanish food is a collection of local traditions rather than a single famous dish.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is paella made from?

Rice, stock, saffron, and a mix of vegetables, seafood, or meat, cooked in a wide, shallow pan. The rice is not stirred, so a crisp layer forms on the bottom.

Is gazpacho healthy?

Yes, very. It is made from raw vegetables, olive oil, and vinegar, and it is rich in vitamins, fibre, and healthy fats, with very few calories.

What are tapas?

Small dishes served with drinks in Spain, meant for sharing. A tapas meal is several small plates on the table, eaten slowly with company.
