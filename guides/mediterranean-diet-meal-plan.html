Mediterranean Diet Meal Plan

A meal plan takes the guesswork out of the Mediterranean diet. This guide gives you a complete seven-day plan, with breakfast, lunch, and dinner for each day, plus the shopping and preparation tips that make it work. Use it as it is, or adapt it to your tastes and your schedule.

The seven-day plan

Monday: Greek yoghurt with honey and walnuts; lentil soup with wholegrain bread; grilled sea bass with roasted vegetables. Tuesday: wholegrain toast with tomato and olive oil; chickpea salad with feta; baked chicken with lemon and potatoes. Wednesday: porridge with figs and almonds; white bean stew with greens; pasta with tomato, garlic, and basil. Thursday: fruit with yoghurt and seeds; tuna and white bean salad; roast vegetables with halloumi. Friday: eggs with spinach and feta; minestrone with wholegrain bread; grilled sardines with salad. Saturday: wholegrain toast with avocado; Greek salad with olives; lamb with rosemary and vegetables. Sunday: yoghurt with berries and nuts; pasta with vegetables and beans; baked fish with lemon and herbs.

Shopping for the week

Shop once for the whole week, using the plan as your list. Buy vegetables that will last — carrots, cabbage, potatoes, and onions — and use delicate greens and tomatoes early in the week. Buy fish for Monday and Friday, and cook it the day you buy it. Keep tinned beans, lentils, and tomatoes in the cupboard, and frozen vegetables as a backup. A single weekly shop, planned from the meal plan, is cheaper and faster than daily trips, and it means you always have the ingredients for dinner.

Preparation and batch cooking

Spend an hour on Sunday preparing for the week. Cook a large pot of lentil soup and a pot of white beans, roast a tray of vegetables, and cook a pot of grains. Wash salad leaves and chop vegetables, and store them in containers. Make a jar of dressing — olive oil, lemon, mustard, and herbs — so salads take seconds to assemble. With this preparation, weekday cooking is reduced to grilling fish, assembling salads, and reheating soup, which takes twenty minutes or less.

Adapting the plan

Adapt the plan to your life. Swap fish for chicken or legumes if you prefer, use seasonal vegetables, and change the grains to suit your cupboard. If you eat out one night, move that day's dinner to another day. If you are cooking for one, halve the recipes and freeze the extra portions. The plan is a template, not a prescription — the pattern matters more than any single meal. After a week, keep the meals you liked, drop the ones you did not, and build your own rotating plan from your favourites.

Meal planning for the month

A weekly plan is good, but a monthly rotation is even better. Write down four weekly plans, one for each week of the month, and rotate them. Each week should have a different set of meals, so you do not get bored, but the structure stays the same: a curry night, a pasta night, a soup night, a fish night, and a roast night. Keep a list of the meals that worked and the ones that did not, and adjust the rotation each month. A monthly plan takes an hour to write, but it saves hours of decisions across the month, and it makes shopping and cooking almost automatic. After three months, you will have a set of plans you can reuse for a year.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I repeat meals in the plan?

Yes. Repeating a favourite breakfast or lunch is fine and makes planning easier. Variety across the week matters more than variety every single day.

How do I adapt the plan for vegetarians?

Replace fish and meat with legumes, eggs, tofu, or halloumi. The plan already includes several vegetarian days, and the structure works the same way.

What if I do not like fish?

Use chicken, eggs, or legumes instead. Fish is an important part of the pattern, but the diet still works well with other proteins.
