Greek Recipes

Greek cooking is one of the foundations of the Mediterranean diet, and its recipes are among the most loved in the world. They are built from olive oil, lemon, garlic, herbs, vegetables, and feta, and they are generous, colourful, and full of flavour. This guide shares the classic Greek dishes and how to cook them at home.

Greek salad and tzatziki

A proper Greek salad — horiatiki — has tomato, cucumber, red onion, olives, and a slab of feta, dressed with olive oil and oregano. There is no lettuce in a traditional Greek salad. Tzatziki, made from strained yoghurt, cucumber, garlic, and dill, is served with almost everything: grilled meat, vegetables, and bread. Both are quick to make and they appear at most Greek meals. The secret is good ingredients: ripe tomatoes, good olive oil, and real feta cheese. Make the salad just before serving so the vegetables stay crisp.

Moussaka and pastitsio

Moussaka is the most famous Greek baked dish: layers of aubergine, spiced mince, and a creamy béchamel sauce, baked until golden. It takes time, but it is worth it for a special occasion. Pastitsio is similar, with pasta instead of aubergine, and it is often called Greek lasagne. Both are rich, so serve them with a simple salad. For a lighter version, use more vegetables and less meat, or make a vegetarian moussaka with lentils instead of mince. These dishes freeze well, so make a large tray and save half.

Souvlaki and grilled dishes

Souvlaki — small pieces of meat grilled on skewers — is Greece's favourite fast food, and it is easy to make at home. Marinate chicken or pork in olive oil, lemon, garlic, and oregano, thread it onto skewers, and grill for a few minutes per side. Serve in pitta bread with tomato, onion, and tzatziki. Grilled fish, served with lemon and herbs, is another Greek staple, and grilled vegetables with olive oil make a good vegetarian main. The grill is central to Greek cooking, and it keeps the food light and flavourful.

Spanakopita and baked dishes

Spanakopita, a pie of spinach and feta in crisp filo pastry, is a beloved Greek dish that works as a main course or a snack. It is made by layering filo pastry with a filling of spinach, feta, onion, and dill, then baking until golden. A similar pie, tiropita, is filled with cheese. These pies are practical because they can be made ahead and reheated, and they are good for picnics and lunchboxes. Serve them with a salad and a dollop of tzatziki for a complete meal.

Greek food beyond the classics

Beyond the famous dishes, Greek cooking has a rich tradition of vegetable dishes, bean stews, and salads. Gigantes, large beans baked in tomato sauce, are a hearty main dish. Briam, a tray of roasted vegetables with olive oil and herbs, is a summer staple. Fasolada, the bean soup, is often called the national dish of Greece. Dolmades, vine leaves stuffed with rice and herbs, are a delicate starter. These dishes are cheap, healthy, and deeply satisfying, and they show that Greek food is not only about moussaka and souvlaki. Try one new Greek vegetable dish each month, and you will build a repertoire that covers every season.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most famous Greek dish?

Moussaka is probably the most famous, along with Greek salad and souvlaki. All three are built from the classic Greek ingredients: olive oil, lemon, garlic, and herbs.

Is Greek food healthy?

Yes, generally. It is rich in vegetables, legumes, fish, and olive oil, with meat in moderation. Rich dishes like moussaka are best as occasional meals rather than everyday food.

Can I make Greek food vegetarian?

Easily. Many Greek dishes are already vegetarian — spanakopita, Greek salad, bean stews, and stuffed vegetables — and moussaka works well with lentils instead of meat.
